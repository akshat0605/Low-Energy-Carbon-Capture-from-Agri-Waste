# Low-Energy Carbon Capture from Agricultural Waste
### Geopolymer Zeolite Synthesis from Rice Husk Ash for Industrial CO₂ Capture

>**Global Sustainability Challenge '25** — Selected in **Top 35 teams out of 200+** for South Asia Finals  
>**Team ThePowerGrid** | Indian Institute of Technology, Guwahati

---

## Overview

This project develops a **low-cost, low-energy process** to synthesize geopolymer zeolites (NaA/NaX) from rice husk ash — an abundant agricultural waste in Northeast India — and uses them as CO₂ adsorbents for industrial carbon capture.

Captured CO₂ is reused for **concrete curing**, forming a closed circular carbon loop that converts emissions into value-added applications.

---

## Problem

| Issue | Scale |
|---|---|
| India's annual rice husk generation | ~25–30 million tons/year |
| Husk openly burned → CO₂ + particulates | ~33,800 Gg/year crop residue emissions |
| Industrial CO₂ share of India's GHG | ~80% of 2,959 Mt CO₂e (2020) |
| Conventional carbon capture (MEA) | High cost + high energy demand |

Rice husk represents both an **environmental burden** and a **lost material opportunity**. Existing CO₂ capture technologies are capital-intensive and centralized, limiting accessibility for regional or small-scale applications.

---

## Our Solution

A **four-stage modular process** that converts rice husk waste into functional zeolite CO₂ adsorbents:

```
Rice Husk → Rice Husk Ash (RHA) → Sodium Silicate → Zeolite (NaA/NaX) → CO₂ Capture → Concrete Curing
```

### Stage 1 — Rice Husk Ash Preparation
- Rice husk collected from local mills, washed, and oven-dried at 110°C
- Calcined in muffle furnace at 500–600°C for 6 hours
- Produces silica-rich RHA suitable for zeolite synthesis

### Stage 2 — Sodium Silicate Extraction
- RHA reacted with aqueous NaOH solution
- Two systems tested: unseeded and pre-seeded (with zeolite seed)
- Reactions sealed in borosilicate bottles, heated at 100°C for 6 hours
- Output: sodium silicate solution

### Stage 3 — Zeolite Synthesis
- Sodium silicate mixed with sodium aluminate in desired Si/Al ratio
- Hydrothermal synthesis at 100°C for 8 hours
- NaA and NaX geopolymer zeolites formed and settled at bottom
- Product filtered, washed with distilled water, and oven-dried

### Stage 4 — CO₂ Capture, Regeneration & Utilization
- CO₂ captured using zeolite adsorbent beds from industrial flue gas
- Zeolite regenerated via temperature/pressure swing
- Captured CO₂ reused for concrete curing (closed carbon loop)

## Key Results

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Synthesis temperature | ~100°C (vs 200–300°C conventional) |
| Energy reduction vs traditional methods | **~50%** |
| Zeolite yield | **76.8%** (7.3g from 4.75g RHA) |
| CO₂ adsorption capacity | **3.5 mmol/g zeolite** |
| CO₂ capture potential | **0.23 g CO₂ per gram precursor** |
| Zeolite formation confirmed via | Limewater qualitative test |

---

## Why This Matters

**vs Conventional Zeolites:**

| Parameter | Traditional Zeolite | Rice Husk Geopolymer Zeolite |
|---|---|---|
| Raw Material | Synthetic silica | Rice Husk Ash (waste) |
| Synthesis Temp | 200–300°C | ~100°C |
| Cost | High | Low |
| Sustainability | Moderate | Excellent |
| CO₂ Adsorption | High | Comparable or better |
| Regeneration Energy | High | Low |
