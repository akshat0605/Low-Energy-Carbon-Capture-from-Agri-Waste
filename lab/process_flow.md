## Stage 1 — Rice Husk Ash (RHA) Formation

**Objective:** Convert raw rice husk into silica-rich ash suitable for zeolite synthesis.

### Steps:
1. Collect raw rice husk from local rice mills.
2. Wash the rice husk thoroughly with water to remove dust and surface contaminants.
3. Spread washed husk on trays and place in hot air oven.
4. Dry at **110°C** until completely moisture-free (approx. 2–3 hours).
5. Transfer dried husk to crucibles and place in muffle furnace.
6. Calcine at **500–600°C for 6 hours**.
7. Allow to cool to room temperature inside furnace.
8. Collect the resulting white/grey powder — this is **Rice Husk Ash (RHA)**.
9. Store RHA in sealed container away from moisture.

### Expected Output:
- White to light grey amorphous powder
- High amorphous silica content (~85–90% SiO₂)

> ⚠️ **Safety Note:** Use heat-resistant gloves and tongs when handling furnace. Operate muffle furnace in ventilated area.

---

## Stage 2 — Sodium Silicate Extraction

**Objective:** Extract soluble silica from RHA using alkaline treatment.

### Steps:
1. Weigh out required amount of RHA (e.g., 4.75 g for lab-scale).
2. Prepare aqueous NaOH solution (concentration per experimental design).
3. Add RHA to NaOH solution in borosilicate reagent bottle.
4. Prepare **two parallel systems:**
   - **System A (Unseeded):** RHA + NaOH solution only
   - **System B (Pre-seeded):** RHA + NaOH solution + small amount of zeolite seed crystal
5. Stir each mixture on magnetic stirrer until uniform.
6. Seal the reagent bottles tightly.
7. Place sealed bottles in oven at **100°C for 6 hours**.
8. Remove and allow to cool.
9. Collect the resulting **sodium silicate solution**.

### Expected Output:
- Clear to slightly yellow sodium silicate (water glass) solution
- Silica successfully dissolved into alkaline medium

---

## Stage 3 — Zeolite Synthesis (Hydrothermal)

**Objective:** React sodium silicate with sodium aluminate to form NaA/NaX zeolites.

### Steps:
1. Prepare sodium aluminate (NaAlO₂) solution in distilled water.
2. Mix sodium silicate solution with sodium aluminate solution in the desired **Si/Al molar ratio**.
3. Stir mixture thoroughly to obtain a uniform gel.
4. Transfer gel to sealed borosilicate reaction vessel.
5. Place in oven and carry out **hydrothermal synthesis at 100°C for 8 hours**.
6. After synthesis, observe white crystalline precipitate settled at the bottom — this is the zeolite.
7. Carefully decant the supernatant liquid.
8. Filter the solid product using Buchner funnel and filter paper.
9. Wash the collected solid repeatedly with distilled water until wash water is neutral (pH ~7).
10. Transfer washed zeolite to crucible.
11. Dry in oven at ~80–100°C for 6 hours.
12. Weigh the final dry zeolite powder and record.

### Expected Output:
- White crystalline powder: NaA and/or NaX geopolymer zeolite
- Characteristic zeolite crystal morphology (cubic for NaA, octahedral for NaX)

---

## Stage 4 — CO₂ Adsorption Confirmation (Limewater Test)

**Objective:** Qualitatively confirm that synthesized material is zeolite with CO₂ adsorption capability.

### Principle:
CO₂ reacts with limewater (Ca(OH)₂) to form white calcium carbonate precipitate:
```
CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃↓ (white/milky) + H₂O
```
If zeolite adsorbs CO₂, less CO₂ is available to react with limewater → delayed milkiness.

### Steps:
1. Prepare fresh limewater solution (saturated Ca(OH)₂ in distilled water).
2. Set up **two identical systems** in separate beakers:
   - **System A:** Limewater + synthesized zeolite
   - **System B:** Limewater + rice husk ash (RHA) — control
3. Place both beakers on magnetic stirrers.
4. Stir both systems uniformly at the same speed.
5. Observe and record the time taken for each system to turn milky/white.
6. Allow to settle for **60 minutes**.
7. Compare turbidity and milkiness of both systems.

### Expected Observation:
| System | Expected Behaviour | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| System B (RHA) | Turns milky **rapidly** | CO₂ freely available; no adsorption |
| System A (Zeolite) | Turns milky with **delay** | CO₂ being adsorbed by zeolite |

### Result Interpretation:
- **Delayed milkiness in System A = successful zeolite formation confirmed**
- The delay indicates CO₂ is being captured by the zeolite pores before it can react with limewater

## Yield Calculation

```
Theoretical Yield = mass of RHA × 2
                  = 4.75 g × 2 = 9.5 g

Percentage Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100
                 = (7.3 / 9.5) × 100
                 = 76.8%
```

### Common Causes of Yield Loss:
- Incomplete crystallization during hydrothermal synthesis
- Fine zeolite particles lost with filtrate during washing
- Material adhering to vessel walls and equipment
- Handling losses during transfer and drying

## Safety Notes

- Handle NaOH with gloves and eye protection — corrosive
- Muffle furnace operates at high temperatures — use proper PPE
- Ensure reagent bottles are not completely sealed airtight during heating (pressure build-up risk)
- Dispose of filtrate and wash water as per laboratory chemical waste protocol
