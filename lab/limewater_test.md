# Limewater Test — CO₂ Adsorption Confirmation

**Team:** ThePowerGrid, IIT Guwahati  
**Purpose:** Qualitative confirmation of zeolite formation and CO₂ adsorption capability

---

## Background

The limewater test is a classic qualitative method to detect CO₂. When CO₂ is bubbled into limewater (saturated Ca(OH)₂ solution), it reacts to form insoluble white calcium carbonate:

```
CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ ↓ (white precipitate) + H₂O
```

The key insight: if zeolite is present and actively adsorbing CO₂, **less CO₂ is available** to react with the limewater — resulting in a **delayed or reduced milky appearance** compared to a control without zeolite.

---

## Test Design

Two parallel systems were prepared and compared:

| System | Contents | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **System A** | Limewater + synthesized zeolite | Test sample |
| **System B** | Limewater + rice husk ash (RHA) | Control (no zeolite) |

---

## Procedure

1. Prepare fresh limewater (saturated Ca(OH)₂ solution in distilled water).
2. Add equal volumes of limewater to two identical beakers.
3. Add synthesized zeolite to **Beaker A**.
4. Add equivalent mass of RHA to **Beaker B** (control).
5. Place both beakers on magnetic stirrers simultaneously.
6. Stir both systems uniformly at the same speed.
7. Observe and note time taken for milkiness to appear in each beaker.
8. Allow both systems to settle undisturbed for **60 minutes**.
9. Photograph and record observations.

---

## Observations

| Observation | System A (Zeolite) | System B (RHA/Control) |
|---|---|---|
| Initial appearance | Clear | Clear |
| Onset of milkiness | **Delayed** | **Rapid** |
| Milkiness at 60 min | Moderate white turbidity | Strongly milky/white |
| Interpretation | CO₂ adsorbed by zeolite → less CO₂ available for reaction | CO₂ freely available → fast reaction with limewater |

---

## Result

✅ **Zeolite formation confirmed**

The delayed milkiness in System A directly indicates that the synthesized material is **adsorbing CO₂** from the atmosphere/solution, reducing its availability for reaction with limewater.

The RHA control (System B) turned milky rapidly because RHA has no significant CO₂ adsorption capability — CO₂ was immediately available to react with limewater.

---

## Significance

This test, while qualitative, provides:
1. **Proof of zeolite formation** — only a porous crystalline material with appropriate pore geometry and surface chemistry would adsorb CO₂ at ambient conditions
2. **Proof of CO₂ adsorption** — the delayed limewater reaction is direct evidence of CO₂ capture
3. **Differentiation from precursor** — confirms the synthesis step successfully converted RHA into a new material with distinct adsorption properties

---

## Limitations

- This is a **qualitative test only** — it confirms adsorption occurs but does not quantify capacity
- Quantitative characterization (BET surface area, CO₂ adsorption isotherms) requires:
  - Gas adsorption analyzer
  - Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)
  - Temperature-programmed desorption (TPD)
- XRD analysis required to confirm crystal phase (NaA vs NaX vs amorphous)
