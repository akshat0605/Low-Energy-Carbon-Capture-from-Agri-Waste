# Yield Calculations — Zeolite Synthesis from Rice Husk Ash

**Team:** ThePowerGrid, IIT Guwahati  
**Experiment:** Lab-scale NaA/NaX Zeolite Synthesis

---

## Experimental Inputs

| Component | Quantity | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Rice Husk Ash (RHA) | 4.75 g | Silica source (limiting reagent) |
| NaOH solution | 145 mL | Alkaline medium / silica extraction |
| Sodium aluminate solution | 145 mL | Aluminium source |

> NaOH and sodium aluminate were used in excess to ensure complete reaction of the RHA silica.

---

## Theoretical Yield Calculation

The conversion factor for RHA → Zeolite is based on the incorporation of aluminium and sodium into the aluminosilicate framework:

```
Theoretical Zeolite = RHA mass × 2
                    = 4.75 g × 2
                    = 9.5 g
```

This factor accounts for:
- Silica from RHA (~60% of zeolite mass)
- Aluminium from sodium aluminate incorporated into framework
- Sodium and oxygen contributions to NaA/NaX structure

---

## Experimental Yield

```
Actual zeolite obtained (after drying) = 7.3 g

Percentage Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100
                 = (7.3 / 9.5) × 100
                 = 76.8%
```

---

## Yield Summary

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| RHA input | 4.75 g |
| Theoretical zeolite | 9.5 g |
| Actual zeolite obtained | 7.3 g |
| **Percentage Yield** | **76.8%** |
| Mass loss | 2.2 g (23.2%) |

---

## Analysis of Yield Loss (23.2%)

The yield below 100% is expected for lab-scale synthesis. Contributing factors:

| Loss Source | Description |
|---|---|
| Incomplete crystallization | Not all silica/alumina converted to crystalline zeolite during hydrothermal step |
| Filtration losses | Fine zeolite particles pass through filter paper with filtrate |
| Washing losses | Repeated washing removes some loosely bound surface particles |
| Transfer losses | Material remaining on vessel walls, crucible, and spatulas |
| Unconverted RHA | Some amorphous silica may not fully dissolve or react |

> **Note:** A yield of 76.8% is considered good for lab-scale zeolite synthesis from agricultural waste precursors. Literature reports for similar processes typically range from 60–85%.

---

## CO₂ Adsorption Capacity

Based on laboratory measurements and literature benchmarking for NaA/NaX zeolites:

```
CO₂ adsorption capacity = 3.5 mmol/g zeolite

CO₂ captured per gram of precursor:
= 3.5 mmol/g × (7.3g zeolite / 4.75g RHA) × 44 g/mol CO₂ / 1000
= 3.5 × 1.537 × 0.044
≈ 0.237 g CO₂ per gram precursor
≈ 0.23 g CO₂/g precursor (reported)
```

## Scale-Up Projections

If scaled to **1 ton of RHA input:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| RHA input | 1,000 kg |
| Expected zeolite yield (at 76.8%) | ~768 kg |
| CO₂ capture capacity | ~2,688 kg CO₂ (at 3.5 mmol/g) |
| CO₂ captured per ton precursor | ~230 kg CO₂ |

At pilot scale targeting **1,000 tons CO₂/year:**
- Required zeolite: ~4,348 kg
- Required RHA: ~5,660 kg (~5.66 tons)
- Rice husk needed (at ~22% ash yield): ~25.7 tons rice husk

---

## Notes on Reproducibility

- Yield may vary ±5–8% between batches depending on:
  - NaOH concentration and purity
  - Calcination temperature uniformity
  - Mixing ratio of sodium silicate to aluminate
  - Hydrothermal synthesis time and temperature consistency
- Pre-seeded system (with zeolite seed) generally gives better crystallinity
- Drying temperature should not exceed 120°C to preserve zeolite pore structure
